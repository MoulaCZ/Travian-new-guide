# Chiefing — Conquering Villages

> Chiefing is how you take over another player's (or Natar's) village instead of founding a new one. For a DEF player, the most important use case is **acquiring a second anvil** — ideally a 15c or well-placed cropper that someone else already partially built.

---

## When to Start Thinking About Chiefing

The right moment is usually when you're **setting up your second anvil village** (roughly your 5th–6th settlement). At this point:

- Founding a new 15c is nearly impossible — competitive players already grabbed them
- But 15c villages that were **founded by impatient players who then quit** become available — partially built, abandoned, free to take
- Your first village is the most developed and has the most time to build Academy and Residence to level 20

> 💡 Watch the server map for 15c tiles with activity that suddenly stops. A village that hasn't been updated in days is likely abandoned — check the player's stats.

---

## Requirements for Chiefing

All of these must be true **at the moment the chief wave lands**:

1. **Target is not a capital**
2. **Target has no Residence or Palace** — it must be destroyed (by catapults in the same attack or a prior wave) before loyalty can be reduced
3. **You send a normal attack** — not a raid. A chief sent as a raid does absolutely nothing to loyalty
4. **Your chief survives the battle** — if they die, loyalty is unaffected
5. **You have enough Culture Points** for an additional village — check your Residence/Palace tabs before attacking

---

## How Loyalty Works

![Chiefing loyalty reduction process](images/chiefing-loyalty.svg)

Every village starts at **100% loyalty**. When loyalty hits **0%**, you take the village.

Each chief wave reduces loyalty by a random amount based on your tribe:

| Tribe | Administrator | Reduction per wave |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|
| Romans | Senator | 20–30% |
| Teutons | Chief | 20–25% |
| Gauls | Chieftain | 20–25% |

This means you typically need **4–6 chief waves** to bring loyalty to 0%. Send them in rapid succession — seconds apart — so loyalty doesn't regenerate between hits.

**Loyalty regenerates** at 2 points per Residence/Palace level every 3 hours, once a protective building exists. That's why you destroy it first.

---

## Large Celebration Bonus

> 🟥 **Run a Large Celebration before chiefing** — it adds **+5% loyalty reduction per administrator wave**.

If the defender also runs a Large Celebration, the effects cancel out. If only you have one running — every chief wave hits harder.

Coordinate with your Town Hall timing. Queue the Large Celebration so it's active when your waves land.

---

## Ask Offensive Players for Help

Most offensive players in your alliance keep **3 administrators trained and ready** at all times — it's part of their standard setup. They can send additional chief waves in coordination with yours, stacking loyalty reduction faster and reducing the number of waves you need.

→ Ping your alliance offensive coordinator before attempting a chief. They'll usually help if you've found a good target.

## Step-by-Step: Chiefing a Village

> As a DEF player you will typically have **1 chief** available, or up to 3 if you've built a Palace to level 20 and haven't used the expansion slots. Offensive allies cover the bulk of the chief waves — coordinate before you start.

1. **Scout the target** — confirm no Residence/Palace, check troop count, check if it's a capital
2. **Confirm you have enough CP** — open your Residence/Palace and check the expansion slots
3. **Ping your offensive coordinator** — tell them the target, share scout results, agree on timing
4. **Clear the village** — catapults target the Residence/Palace, troops kill the defenders, rams for the wall if it's high level. All as a normal attack.
5. **Ally sends all their chiefs**
6. **You send all your chiefs in one attack, timed to arrive 1–2 minutes after the ally's attack** — this gap is intentional. Starting loyalty can be 100%, 125%, or lower — nobody knows in advance. If the ally's attack accidentally reduces loyalty to 0 and they take the village, they have those 1–2 minutes to see it and recall their troops before your attack lands. Your attack then takes the village from them — they lose one chief, their army gets home safely.
   > 💡 If this happens, ask whether you should send resources to cover that lost chief. They helped you get the village — a small thank-you is good alliance etiquette.

7. **Start a Large Celebration** — time it to be active when your attacks land
8. **Secure the village** — see After the Conquest below

## After the Conquest

Do these **immediately** — in this order:

1. **Build a Residence or Palace right away.** Until you do, the village has no loyalty protection and anyone else can chief it out from under you. This is the very first thing you build.

2. **Withdraw all your troops — chiefs and escorts.** Pull everything out immediately after the conquest. The village is fresh and undefended, but that's intentional for now — see step 3.

3. **Wait for incoming attacks before defending.** The previous owner almost certainly had this village on other players' farm lists. Those players have attacks in flight that were planned against the old owner — not against you. Don't garrison troops until the last of those attacks has landed. Killing someone's raid party when they had no idea the village changed hands is bad etiquette — they weren't attacking you.

4. **Message players who were farming this village.** Once you've spotted the incoming attacks in your rally point, message those players. Let them know the village is under new ownership and ask them to remove it from their farm list. Most will respect this — it's standard etiquette.

5. **Send resources and a trade route.** The village has nothing. Ship food and building materials from your other villages immediately so you can start building.

6. **Send defensive reinforcements once the last farm attack has cleared.** Only then garrison troops — now you know anyone attacking is targeting you, not the old owner.

Other things to know:
- The village **resets**: academy research, smithy upgrades, walls, and tribe buildings are gone
- Oases detach and become independent — you'll need to re-attach them once you have a Hero's Mansion

## Special Case: Taking a 15c as Your Second Anvil

If you're lucky enough to find an abandoned or weakly held 15c, treat it as a priority target. After taking it:

> 🟥 **Build a Palace here, not a Residence** — this makes it your capital village.

Why this matters:
- Resource fields in the **capital can exceed level 10** — on a 15c, this is the entire point
- With a Palace at level 20, you can train **3 administrators** here and use expansion slots to take over **3 more villages** that are already partly built
- A 15c with fields pushed to level 12+ feeds an enormous army

### Build order after taking a 15c

1. Build **Palace** (instead of Residence)
2. Push all 15 croplands as high as possible (level 12+ with Palace)
3. **Bakery → 5** for grain bonus
4. Granaries × 3 → 20
5. Palace → 20 (unlocks 3 administrator training slots for future chiefs)
6. Follow the rest of the standard [Anvil build](second-village.md)

> This village becomes your new capital and the engine of your account. Protect it at all costs — it cannot be chiefed while it has a Palace.

### What if there's no 15c to chief when you're ready for your second anvil?

No problem — don't wait indefinitely. Two options:

1. **Found a standard 3339 anvil** as your second anvil now (as described in the [Second Village guide](second-village.md)), and keep watching the map. If a 15c opens up later, chief it then and convert it to your capital at that point.

2. **Start a new group of villages** with the 15c as the second village in that group: settle a feeder village first (you need it for resources anyway), then chief the 15c as the anvil for that group. This requires the 15c to still be available and takes more planning — but if the tile is good, it's worth it.

Founding a 15c from scratch (with settlers) is also possible but very resource-intensive and slow. Chiefing a partially developed one is almost always better if the opportunity exists.

## Protecting Your Own Villages from Being Chiefed

- **Always have a Residence or Palace** in every village — loyalty cannot be reduced while one exists
- **Run Large Celebrations** during active ops — reduces enemy chief effectiveness by 5%
- **Use Tablets of Law** — each tablet adds 1% loyalty (up to 125%), forcing attackers to send more waves
- **Keep scouts in all villages** — early warning of incoming attacks
- **Ask for alliance reinforcements** — a defended village with troops makes clearing much harder
