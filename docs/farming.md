# Farming — Raiding Inactive Villages

> Farming is the act of repeatedly raiding inactive or weak villages to steal their resources. Done consistently, it's the single biggest multiplier on your economy — far outpacing what your own fields produce, especially early server.

---

## Why Farming Matters

Your resource fields grow slowly. A level 5 cropland produces maybe 100 grain per hour. An active farmer with 100 Clubswingers hitting 30–50 targets can bring in tens of thousands of resources per day — **resources that go directly into more troops, which farm more villages, which fund more troops**.

The snowball is real. A player who starts farming in week 1 will typically have 3–5× more army than a pure builder by week 3. The army then defends better, deters attacks, and keeps the loop going.

**As a DEF player**, farming isn't your main focus — but it still matters. Farm income funds your barracks queue between feeder shipments, keeps your hero levelled, and gives you flexibility when the alliance needs a quick response. Ignore it and you'll always be resource-starved.

## F2P vs. Gold Club

> 🟥 **Farm List is a Gold Club feature.** Without it, you send every raid manually through the Rally Point — one attack at a time. With Gold Club (200 Gold, one-time purchase), you can send all raids with a single click.

This is one of the most impactful Gold purchases in the game. A Gold Club player with 40 farms clicks once. A free player does the same 40 attacks individually — taking 5–10 minutes per cycle. At 6 cycles a day, that's an hour of clicking vs. six clicks.

**If you buy one Gold feature, make it this.** Everything else is comfort. Farm List is leverage.

For F2P before Gold Club: keep a list of target coordinates. Hit them one by one from the Rally Point during your normal play sessions. Focus on the closest targets — long-distance manual raiding isn't worth the time.

## Finding Farms

You're looking for villages that:
- Have **low or static population** — no growth = inactive player
- Have **no alliance tag** — unprotected, no retaliation risk
- Are **close to your village** — fewer fields = faster round trips = more resources per day
- Exited **beginner's protection recently** — warehouse full, no cranny yet, prime target

> ⚠️ **Watch out:** A village with population 50 might be a secondary village of a top-100 player. Always check the player's profile before farming them repeatedly. Provoking a large alliance member can result in catapult strikes on your fields.

### External tools

These tools find inactive players around your coordinates automatically:

**[GetterTools](https://www.gettertools.com/en/)** — filter by radius, inactivity (days without population change), travel time per troop type. Click a result → goes straight to your Rally Point.

**[TravcoTools](https://travcotools.com/en/)** — filter by days inactive, population range, alliance whitelist/blacklist. Shows "In farmlist / Not in farmlist" status so you know what you've already added.

Both are officially endorsed by Travian. Use them at the start of each session to refresh your target list.

## Scouting Before You Raid

**Never blind-raid a village you haven't scouted** — unless its population is under 10, in which case sending 2–3 troops to test is low-risk.

Scouts reveal troop counts and whether the hero is present. If your scout dies, the village has scouts defending → active player → skip it.

**Protocol:**
1. Send 1 scout to any new potential farm
2. Scout returns alive + 0 defenders → add to farm list
3. Scout dies → do not raid, remove from list
4. Use the in-game **Combat Simulator** if you see any defenders — only attack if expected losses are worth the loot

Build scouts as your second unit type (after your main raider). This is not optional.

## Which Troops to Use

The best raiding troop depends on your tribe. Key stats: **carry capacity** (resources per troop) and **speed** (fields per hour — determines round-trip time).

| Tribe | Unit | Carry | Speed | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|-------|-------|
| Teutons | Clubswinger | 60 | 7 f/h | Best early raider — cheap, fast to mass-produce |
| Teutons | Teuton Knight | 80 | 12 f/h | Mid-game upgrade, higher carry |
| Gauls | Theutates Thunder | 75 | 19 f/h | Best speed; excellent for nearby farms |
| Romans | Equites Imperatoris | 100 | 14 f/h | High carry, expensive — not ideal for massing |
| Romans | Legionnaire | 50 | 6 f/h | Poor raider — slow, low carry, avoid if possible |

**Teutons** are the best farming tribe overall. Clubswinger is the most efficient early raider by cost-to-carry ratio — mass-produce them immediately. The Teuton hero also partially bypasses cranny protection and adds +20% plunder.

**Gauls** farm efficiently mid-game thanks to Theutates Thunder's speed. Fast round trips mean more cycles per day.

**Romans** are the weakest farming tribe. EI works for heavy established farms but is expensive to train. If you play Roman, compensate by targeting very close, very inactive villages.

## Raiding Mechanics

**Cranny** — every village can build a Cranny that hides resources from raiders. Level 1 protects 200 per resource type (800 total). Level 10 protects 2,000 per type (8,000 total). Gauls get 50% bonus cranny protection. **Teuton hero reduces enemy cranny protection by 20%**.

**Bounty** — what your troops bring home. Capped by troop carry capacity. Resources in a hero's inventory cannot be stolen. If your warehouse is full when troops return, **excess loot is lost** — keep storage upgraded.

**Scaling up** — if your last raid returned 100% full carry (troops came home full), you're leaving resources on the table. Increase troop count by ~20% and repeat until returns drop below 100%. This finds the optimal number of troops per target.

**Raid frequency** — no game limit on how often you can raid the same village. Practically: raid often enough that the target never accumulates enough to fill their cranny. For close farms (under 10 fields), a fast cavalry unit can hit the same target 3–4 times per hour. For infantry hitting a farm 15 fields away, once every 4–5 hours is more realistic.

**Empty warehouse returns nothing** — if you've stripped a farm and raid it again immediately, your troops return empty-handed. Stagger your timing so resources have time to accumulate.

## Building and Managing Your Farm List

1. **Find targets** — GetterTools or TravcoTools, filtered by radius and inactivity
2. **Scout** — send one scout per new target before adding
3. **Add to list** — Rally Point → Farm List tab; assign minimum troops needed to fill carry on a typical raid
4. **Send All** — one click raids every farm in the list simultaneously
5. **Review regularly** — farms that return 0 resources 2–3 times in a row should be removed. Either the target quit, built crannies, or started defending

> 💡 Organise multiple lists: "Close infantry farms" (fast cycle), "Cavalry radius" (wider area), "Oases" (separate). Sending all at once from each list keeps your troops busy continuously.

## Oasis Farming

Oases also produce resources and can be raided. Unlike player villages, they **always have animals defending** — the type and number depends on the oasis type and how long since it was last raided (animals respawn over time).

- **Safe to farm:** oases with small animals (rabbits, foxes, deer)
- **Dangerous:** oases with elephants, tigers, or bears — these kill infantry fast
- Always check the oasis before farming it with cavalry (faster, more survivable)
- Oases attached to your village via Hero's Mansion **cannot be raided by others** — only by you

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts |
|---------|-------------|
| Not scouting first | One unexpected defender stack wipes your early army |
| Sending too many troops to one target | Troops still in transit = not farming elsewhere |
| Sending too few and getting wiped | Losing 50 Clubswingers early sets you back days |
| Raiding allied players | Instant drama, possible retaliation, broken trust |
| Ignoring distance | A 30-field farm visited every 10 hours is barely worth the slot |
| Not refreshing the list | Dead farms still in your list = missed cycles on live ones |
| Warehouse full on return | Loot silently disappears — always keep storage ahead of income |
| Starting too late | Players exiting beginner's protection are the richest targets — hit them immediately |
