# Tips & Tricks

A collection of advanced tips, lesser-known mechanics, and common wisdom from experienced Travian players.

---

## Economy

- **Always queue a building.** If your queue is empty, you're wasting time. Even level 1 buildings count toward population and development.
- **Upgrade Warehouse/Granary before you need it.** Don't wait until it's full — upgrade when you're at 70% capacity.
- **Trade excess resources immediately.** If one resource is capped and others aren't, trade 1:1 with an ally. Overflow is waste.
- **Oasis > field level at high levels.** Upgrading a crop field from 9 to 10 is very expensive. A 25% oasis on that village is often more efficient.
- **Market routes between villages.** Once you have 2+ villages, set up automatic recurring trades to balance resources.

---

## Military

- **Never send your entire army.** Keep 20–30 units home as a trip wire — if someone attacks while your army is away, you'll at least get a combat report.
- **Scout everything before a real attack.** A hidden Gaul trapper or a full defense will destroy an unscoutted attack.
- **Ram the wall first.** Send a small Ram wave timed to arrive seconds before your main army. Even partially destroying the wall reduces enemy defense bonus significantly.
- **Fake attacks are free information warfare.** Send 1 unit as an attack. The defender sees an incoming attack and panics — they may reinforce, revealing if they have strong allies nearby.
- **Catapult targeting.** In the Workshop, you can set catapult targets (random or specific building). In endgame, target: Main Building (slows construction), Granary (starves troops), or the WW building directly.
- **Hero always goes with attacks.** In early-mid game, a high-level hero with good items can turn a losing battle into a win. Don't leave them home if you're raiding.

## Raiding

- **Build your farm list from day 1.** Every inactive you raid should go on the list. By week 2, you should have 10–20 reliable farms.
- **Raid in waves.** Don't send 100 troops to one farm — send 10 troops to 10 farms. More total loot, less wasted capacity.
- **Check farm activity.** If a "farm" suddenly has troops, they've returned. Scout before your next raid.
- **Raiding times matter.** Resources regenerate over time — raid a village right after their production cycle peaks (overnight raids often return more resources).
- **Abandoned villages** (player quit the server) are often the best farms — no troops, full storage, no risk.

## Hero

- **Level hero as fast as possible.** Each adventure gives XP; early adventures also give key items (bonus resources, equipment).
- **Don't let hero die.** A dead hero drops their equipment and you lose it. Bring them home if you're not sure of a battle outcome.
- **Equip for role.** Raiding hero = attack bonus items. Defensive hero = defense items. Endgame hammer hero = best attack items available.
- **Hero production.** The Hero's Mansion can also generate resources (at the cost of hero health). In a crisis, this can bridge a resource gap.

---

## Village Management

- **Specialize villages early.** Don't try to do everything in one village. One village = resource production. Second village = army. Third village = buffer/expansion.
- **Capital village choice matters.** Your capital cannot be conquered — it's permanent. Once you've decided, develop it fully. Many players set a 15-crop or 9-crop as capital.
- **Population ≠ strength.** High population just means you built many things. A low-pop player with a smart army can be much more dangerous than a high-pop builder.
- **Demolish wisely.** You can demolish buildings to free up slots but it's slow and expensive. Plan your village layout before building.

---

## Timing & Activity

- **Log in at off-hours.** Many players only play during business hours. Attacking or raiding at night or early morning catches people offline and unable to reinforce.
- **Coordinate attacks to the second.** Travian combat is resolved when troops **arrive**, not when they're sent. Calculate travel times and sync with allies.
- **Use the in-game clock.** All times in Travian are server time. Know your offset to avoid mistakes.
- **Don't go offline during war.** If your alliance is at war, try to log in every few hours. Attacks can come at any time.

## Common Traps for New Players

| Trap | Better approach |
|------|----------------|
| Building every available building | Focus on key buildings; quality over quantity |
| Training weak units late-game | Upgrade Smithy; quality troops beat quantity |
| Ignoring Smithy | Always be researching unit upgrades |
| Forgetting to check crop | Check crop balance every login |
| Not using farm list | Farm list is essential — use it from week 1 |
| Attacking too early | Scout first, attack second |
| Hoarding resources | Spend them — they can be raided; buildings can't |
| Trusting everyone | Even "friendly" players can betray — protect your hammer location |

## Quality of Life Tips

- **Browser notifications** — Enable them so you get alerted when attacks are incoming.
- **Travian Plus / Gold** — Gold features (NPC trader, auto-extend build queue) are quality-of-life but not required to be competitive.
- **NPC Trader** — Converts excess resources to needed ones at a 3:2 ratio. Very useful when you're blocked by one resource type.
- **Save coordinates.** Write down your own village coordinates and key ally/enemy coordinates — you'll need them for attack calculations.
- **Use third-party tools.** The Travian community has calculators for: travel time, battle simulator, crop calculators. Search "Travian tools" for options.

---

## Mindset

- **Losing troops is part of the game.** Don't be afraid to attack. You learn more from a failed attack than from never attacking.
- **One bad day doesn't end your server.** You can always recover from a bad attack or a raid loss — as long as your economy is intact.
- **Play with friends.** Travian is dramatically more fun and more competitive with real-life friends in the same alliance.
- **Accept the time commitment.** 1x speed servers last 12 months. Set expectations early — it's a marathon, not a sprint.
